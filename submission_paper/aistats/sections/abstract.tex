A cell's measured transcriptome in targeted in situ spatial transcriptomics mixes three things: what the cell intrinsically is, how it responds to its microenvironment, and transcripts that were assigned to it but belong to its neighbours. The second and third are confounded at first order, since both make a cell's expression predictable from its neighbours' types, so a spatial effect read from a single section is only partially identified against leakage. We make that dependence explicit. The data bound the leak fraction from above but never from below, so we do not estimate it: DISCELL, a variational model that splits a cell's counts into an intrinsic latent state $\vz$, a latent response $\vw$ tied to a deterministic description of its microenvironment, and a leakage mixture over graph neighbours governed by a leak fraction $\kappa$, is refitted on a grid of $\kappa$, and every claimed spatial contrast is reported with its breakdown point, the smallest leak fraction that explains it away.
