\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\readorder{3}

DISCELL writes a cell's counts as an intrinsic state, a response to its microenvironment and leakage from its neighbours, and makes explicit what the data leave open between the last two. The intrinsic state is pushed toward invariance to the niche given the cell's type, by architecture and by an adversary, and a held-out probe, independent of training, measures what remains: on four sections, a few percent of the within-type variance of neighbour composition, less than resolVI and Cellina leave on three of them, while the intrinsic state keeps the cell type and the cell cycle. MintFlow leaves less still, with an intrinsic latent that keeps almost no within-type state. No comparison method beats DISCELL on both composition leakage and retained cycle state on any section: those that leak less keep less, and those that keep more leak more (\cref{fig:tradeoff}). The leakage enters through a fixed kernel on the section's own graph. The leak fraction is not estimated, since the data bound it only from above (\cref{prop:kappa-bound}), but swept, and every claimed spatial contrast is reported with the leak fraction at which it breaks down; all hold across the grid except the response's lean toward signalling genes on the fresh-frozen section and the transported prediction's margin over its leakage part on the ovarian FFPE and lung sections, while its margin over its programme part, zero by construction where no leakage is modelled, is reported as a trajectory: positive in every seed on every section from $\kappa = 0.05$ to $0.2$, and negative at the largest leak fractions on the primary section. At the operating point the per-cell channel of the response is closed by the weight on its divergence, so what the response carries there is largely a regression on the context; only on the lung section does it separate some merged clusters better than that regression.

The model is also fast to fit. With the whole section on one GPU, a DISCELL fit takes $1.3$ to $43$ minutes, its evaluations every five epochs and the early stop included, on sections of about seventy thousand to over a million cells, at a peak memory of $1.4$ to $15.1$ GiB (\cref{tab:timing}). That is faster than resolVI's training alone on every section ($5.5$ to $105$ minutes) and than Cellina's on three of the four; Cellina is comparably fast ($3.4$ to $29$ minutes) and the fastest on the fresh-frozen section. The two other models that split a cell into an intrinsic and a context latent are far costlier: MintFlow trains for $9$ to $16$ hours where it could be run and could not be run on the million-cell fresh-frozen section for lack of host memory, and SIMVI, which trains for over three hours on the TMA core, ran out of memory on a 24\,GB GPU on the three larger sections (\cref{tab:baselines}).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The leakage has one form, a section-wide fraction that is the same for every gene, with no ambient term, and the sweep separates a spatial association only from leakage of that form, not from spatially organised intrinsic variation within a type nor from cells selecting their niche; a fixed false-positive floor alone lowers the primary section's transport read from $0.65$ to $0.50$. The decomposition is relative to labels derived from the same contaminated counts, and the invariance is incomplete: a nonlinear probe still recovers $3.5$ to $5.5\%$ of the within-type variance of neighbour composition from the intrinsic state. At the operating point the response's per-cell channel is closed by design, not by the data, so its readouts are largely readouts of the context regression, and several of them are not specific to it. In simulation the intrinsic encoder's posterior mean differs measurably from the exact one, and nothing beyond the bound on the leak fraction and the graded invariance guarantees identification. Every fit is to one section, with one section held out whole, and the comparison methods were run at their published defaults while DISCELL was calibrated on these sections. The full list of limitations, with what each one implies, is in \cref{app:limitations}.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Three extensions follow directly: a perturbed and an unperturbed condition, which would add a perturbation response as a third source of variation, with the leakage sweep applied to all three; an operating point with an open per-cell response channel, for per-cell anomaly scores and counterfactuals with abduction; and a nuclear/extranuclear split of the transcripts, which would supply the gene a cell cannot express itself that the upper bound needs to become an estimate of the leak fraction (\cref{prop:kappa-bound}). Other assays need counts assigned to segmented cells, one position per cell, a type label and an image descriptor; what that requires on other imaging platforms, on sequencing-based arrays and for protein imaging is discussed, untested, in \cref{app:inputs}.
