\subsection{Lineage Labels}
\label{app:lineage}

\flag{REVIEWER}{keep: label mappings and judgement calls; reviewers will check them} The fitted labels are lineage-level on every section (\cref{sec:setting}). States, such as proliferating, activated or hypoxic, and locations, such as tumour- or stroma-associated, are merged into their lineage; lineages and developmentally distinct sub-lineages are kept. The mappings were fixed and recorded before any model was refitted.

\paragraph{Curated annotations.}
On the ovarian FFPE section the eighteen curated classes become twelve (\cref{tab:lineage-ovarian}). Two classes needed a decision. The class named as a SOX2-OT$^+$ tumour subtype is mostly not tumour: its cells have a median of $27$ transcripts against $333$ for tumour cells, three quarters show no SOX2-OT count, and its pooled profile decomposes mostly into macrophage expression, so it is folded into Unassigned rather than into the tumour lineage. The class named as malignant cells lining a cyst expresses mesothelial markers (CALB2, PRG4, BNC1, PDPN, UPK3B) and lacks the tumour markers (EPCAM, PAX8, ESR1), and becomes its own lineage. On the lung TMA the thirty-five curated classes become thirty: the state classes (activated, inflammatory and myofibroblasts, activated endothelium and proliferating cells) are reassigned cell by cell to the sub-lineage their markers indicate, with the cell-cycle genes left out for the proliferating class, and the other classes are kept.

\paragraph{Other judgement calls.}
Four further choices are not forced by the rule and are stated so that they can be revisited. The tumour- and stroma-associated fibroblasts of the ovarian FFPE section are merged, because what separates them is the cancer-associated fibroblast programme, a state, rather than a lineage. Its two endothelial classes are merged too, although what separates them is less clear: a tip-cell state on one side and a weakly venous identity on the other. On the lung TMA the alveolar and adventitial fibroblasts are kept as distinct sub-lineages, and the myofibroblasts, which could belong to either, are split between them by their markers, about evenly. The cell-by-cell reassignment of the TMA's state classes is expected to be right for about three cells in four, and its margins are recorded with the mapping.

\paragraph{Clustered sections.}
On the lung FFPE section and the fresh-frozen ovarian section the labels start from unsupervised clusters, and each cluster is assigned the lineage its marker genes indicate, on the lung TMA's vocabulary for the lung section (\cref{tab:lineage-clusters}). Clusters of immune cells next to tumour that carry some tumour signal keep their immune lineage, since that signal is the leakage the model is built to absorb, and one low-depth cluster of the fresh-frozen section becomes Unassigned.

\subsection{What the Model Needs}
\label{app:inputs}

\flag{TRIM}{untested-assay discussion; condense to one paragraph} Every fit in this paper is to a section profiled with Xenium Prime 5K. From its output the model uses five inputs (\cref{tab:inputs}). The graph needs positions only. It is the Delaunay adjacency of the cell positions, and the kernel's two ingredients, the length of the shared Voronoi face and the distance between positions, come from the positions too (\cref{app:graph}). The segmented polygons are therefore not required. Our runs take each cell's position as the centroid of its segmented polygon; the centroid the platform reports would serve equally in form. The polygons were used for three things outside the model: choosing the radius of the disc that removes the cell from the image field, which a new tissue or platform would need to recheck from its cell sizes (\cref{app:image}), and the graph and mask comparisons of \cref{app:graph,app:image}. The transcript coordinates, the nucleus boundaries and the H\&E image taken after the run are not used. Cell-cycle genes enter a diagnostic only (\cref{sec:selection}).

\paragraph{Implicit requirements.} Counts must be assigned to segmented cells at single-cell resolution: the leakage term models transcripts misassigned between neighbouring cells, and has no meaning for a unit that holds several cells. One fit is to one contiguous section. Counts are modelled as a multinomial given each cell's total, suited to the hundreds to about a thousand counts per cell of in situ data (\cref{sec:generative}), and a type label must be derivable for every cell. Nothing else is assumed about the gene panel.

\paragraph{Other assays (untested).} None of the following has been run. Each paragraph states what the model's assumptions would require, not what it would deliver.

\emph{Imaging platforms with other panels.} Other imaging-based assays, including smaller Xenium panels, provide segmented counts, positions and fluorescence stains, the inputs of \cref{tab:inputs}. The model applies in form. What changes is the depth and number of genes, and the stains, whose mapping onto the image model's marker vocabulary would have to be redone (\cref{app:image}).

\emph{Sequencing-based arrays.} On a high-resolution array whose bins are assigned to segmented cells, counts per cell and positions are available, but two things differ. The image is H\&E rather than multiplexed fluorescence, so the image descriptor would need an H\&E encoder. And transcripts are also mislocated by lateral movement during capture, a mechanism separate from segmentation whose extent differs between platforms: small on one high-resolution array \citep{deoliveira2025visiumhd}, larger on another \citep{ren2025benchmark}. The form of the leakage kernel would have to be re-examined before the sweep could be read the same way. Arrays whose spots hold several cells pose a deconvolution problem rather than a leakage problem and are outside the model's setting.

\emph{Protein imaging.} Multiplexed protein imaging measures intensities per marker rather than counts, so the multinomial likelihood would have to be replaced. The leakage mechanism carries over, since signal from a neighbour's membrane spills over into the cell, through imperfect segmentation and through the interleaving of adjacent membranes \citep{bai2021redsea}, and the image model was built for such images \citep{kronos2025}, so the image descriptor would be native to the assay.

\subsection{Training Cost}
\label{app:timing}

\Cref{tab:timing} gives the cost of a fit. DISCELL's time per epoch is pure training, measured over twenty epochs with no evaluation. Its wall-clock time covers the three final fits, including evaluations every five epochs and the early stop. The comparison methods' times are the training time of the fits compared in this paper, at lineage labels, as recorded when they were run. MintFlow's whole-section fit of the lung FFPE section trained for $879$ minutes, $14.8$ hours of wall-clock time with its set-up and prediction, against $3.2$ to $7.4$ minutes for a whole DISCELL fit of the same section. Its fit of the ovarian FFPE section trained for $939$ minutes, $15.8$ hours of wall-clock time, with a peak of $9.5$ GB of GPU and $23.6$ GB of host memory. The other whole-section fits of SIMVI and MintFlow could not be run on the resources currently available; the measured reasons are in \cref{tab:baselines,tab:timing}.

In symbols, a training step on a tile of $n$ seeds with $n_1$ cells in ring one costs $O((n + n_1)\,G h)$ in the encoders and decoder, for hidden width $h$, and $O(|E_n|\,G)$ in the foreign influx, for the $|E_n|$ edges into the seeds (\cref{sec:batching}). The six adversary head updates add $O\big(6\,(n + n_1)\,h_a(\dz + K)\big)$ with $h_a = 64$, small beside the gene-sized terms because $\dz$ and $K$ are far below $G$. An epoch visits every training tile once, so its cost is linear in the number of cells; the held-out probes are fitted on at most $30{,}000$ cells whatever the section's size. Peak memory is the section's count matrix, held resident, plus the gathered edge-by-gene rows of the influx, $O(|E_n|\,G)$ per step, which the tile size bounds. The leakage sweep multiplies the cost of a fit by its six grid points and three seeds, eighteen fits per section. Sample size is not analysed: how many cells a section needs for the readouts to stabilise is not studied beyond the range of sizes in \cref{tab:sections}.

\subsection{Planted Worlds}
\label{app:planted}
\readorder{17}

\flag{MERGE}{simulations are split over app:planted and app:synthetic; gather in one appendix} Three questions about the fitting procedure are answered on simulated sections, where the truth is known.

\paragraph{The amortisation gap: what is measured.}
The intrinsic encoder $q(\vz_i \mid \vx_i, t_i)$ reads a cell's counts and type but not the foreign influx $\rhobar_i$, although the counts contain it. Its posterior mean can therefore differ from the exact posterior mean $\E[\vz_i \mid \vx_i, t_i, \rhobar_i]$ that a model told the influx would reach. This difference, the amortisation gap, cannot be measured on tissue, where the exact posterior is unknown, so we measure it on simulated sections for which the exact posterior can be computed.

\paragraph{The simulation.}
Each simulated section follows DISCELL's generative model with the response switched off, so that every quantity lies inside the model class the fit assumes and a gap measured here is a gap of inference, not of model mismatch.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Cells and types.} $3{,}000$ cells at uniformly random positions in a $700\um$ square, and four types laid out in spatial patches: each cell takes the type of the nearest of twelve anchor points, three per type, up to a random perturbation, so that neighbours are often of another type and leakage mixes types, as in tissue.
\item \emph{Intrinsic state and composition.} $\vz_i \sim \N(\mathbf{m}_{t_i}, 0.5^2\vI)$ in two dimensions, with type means $\mathbf{m}_t \sim \N(\mathbf{0}, 1.5^2\vI)$, and a clean composition $\vrho_i = \softmax(\vz_i\mathbf{A})$ over forty genes, with loadings $\mathbf{A}$ drawn from a standard normal.
\item \emph{Leakage.} The Delaunay graph of the positions, pruned at $40\um$, with the kernel $\beta_{ij} \propto \exp(-d_{ij}/\tau)$, $\tau = 20\um$, normalised over each cell's neighbours: the model's own kernel with every face set to one. The leak fraction is $\kappa = 0.2$, and $\rhobar_i = \sum_j \beta_{ij}\vrho_j$.
\item \emph{Counts.} A depth $\ell_i \sim \Pois(D)$, at least $20$, for $D \in \{100, 300, 1{,}000\}$, and $\vx_i \sim \Mult\big(\ell_i, (1-\kappa)\vrho_i + \kappa\rhobar_i\big)$.
\item \emph{Image descriptor.} An eight-dimensional vector made from the cell's neighbour composition plus noise, so that the context has something to read.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The exact posterior.}
Given $\rhobar_i$, $\kappa$, $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{m}_{t_i}$ and $\ell_i$, the posterior of $\vz_i$ is a two-dimensional density. It has no closed form, since the multinomial and the softmax are not conjugate, but in two dimensions its mean and covariance are computed to numerical precision on a $129 \times 129$ grid centred on its mode.

\paragraph{The fit and the comparison.}
DISCELL is fitted to the counts with its response channel present, as on tissue, at the final configuration (\cref{tab:architecture}) with the widths reduced to the small world ($\dz = 2$, $\dw = 2$, hidden width $128$, attention width $16$) and tiles of $512$ cells: the adversary at $\alphaa = 0.3$ with its composition term weighted three times, $\alphaw = 0.1$ with its warm-up over thirty epochs, $\alphaz = \tfrac12/\lbar$ with $\lbar$ the simulated section's median count (about $0.005$, $0.0017$ and $0.0005$ at the three depths), and the budget of $500$ epochs with patience $40$, with $15\%$ of the tiles held out for the stopping rule of \cref{sec:selection}. The posterior means are read at the accepted checkpoint. Each world is fitted at the true leak fraction and at leak fractions off by up to $0.1$ either way, with three seeds, $45$ fits in all; their accepted checkpoints lie between epochs $59$ and $434$. Because $\vz$ is identified only up to a smooth invertible map, the encoder's posterior means are first mapped onto the simulation's coordinates by a small network with five-fold cross-fitting; since the means are two-dimensional, the map cannot add information the encoder did not keep. The gap of a cell is the distance between its mapped mean and the exact mean, divided by the size of the exact posterior's standard deviation, and it is averaged over cells and over three seeds. Four references are fitted to the exact posterior mean directly, also with cross-fitting: a multilayer perceptron given the encoder's inputs, the same perceptron given $\rhobar_i$ as well, which the encoder never sees, a linear regression on the encoder's inputs, and the type mean, which knows no counts.

\paragraph{The result.}
\flag{REVIEWER}{encoder gap exceeds the perceptron's at the final configuration; stated in the limitations} At the true leak fraction the encoder's gap is $0.65$, $1.02$ and $1.93$ posterior standard deviations at the three depths (\cref{tab:planted-gap}). That is above the gap of the perceptron trained directly on the same inputs at every depth ($0.61$, $0.95$ and $1.60$), with the encoder below it in only three of the nine combinations of depth and seed, and $0.12$ to $0.58$ above the gap of the perceptron that also sees $\rhobar_i$; it is well below the linear regression's and the type mean's. The encoder's posterior mean therefore differs from the exact one by a measurable amount, more than a flexible regression on the same inputs leaves, and a regression that also sees the influx comes closer still. The gap grows with depth only in units of the posterior width, because the posterior narrows as the depth increases, from a standard deviation of $0.17$ to $0.06$; in units of $\vz$ itself it stays roughly flat, at $0.16$ to $0.17$. A wrong leak fraction enlarges it by a factor of at most $1.13$ over the mismatches tried, but the gap is not smallest at the true value at any depth: at $D = 1{,}000$, for instance, a leak fraction $0.1$ too low gives $1.77$ against $1.93$. The seed whose fits stop earliest is the worst at every depth; whether the adversary or the stopping rule accounts for the difference from the development configuration has not been tested. At that configuration (the closed-form penalty of \cref{eq:penalty} in place of the adversary, a fixed $600$ epochs with no stopping rule and every tile trained) the encoder's gap was smaller, $0.55$, $0.83$ and $1.48$, and below the perceptron's on the same inputs. The references are regression models of finite capacity, not information bounds, so these statements are comparative.

\paragraph{A leak-subtracted encoder input.}
\flag{TRIM}{development history; tab:deviations2 already carries it in one line} The intrinsic encoder could be given counts from which the expected foreign influx has been subtracted, rather than the raw counts. On planted worlds with a known leak, this helped on one of three worlds. In a follow-up with twelve seeds it reversed on three of the six seeds where the comparison was not saturated, and it cost up to twenty-two points of recall of the planted signal. The encoder therefore reads raw counts, and the subtraction is kept as an option, off by default.

\paragraph{A dead response channel.}
\flag{TRIM}{archived development fits; keep the two checks and the final-fit result} A fit can end with a response that carries no information about the niche: the divergence of $\vw$ to its prior collapses and the context is ignored. Two checks flag it. During training, a summed divergence of $\vw$ below $10^{-5}$ over the first twenty epochs marks the fit. After training, the information between $\vw$ and the niche, on held-out cells and against a within-type permutation floor, must exceed its floor. Among the archived fits of the development period, twelve had a dead channel, nine of them on the deepest section. The KL warm-up of the final configuration removed it in every one of eight refits of that section, and the checks flag no final fit.

\paragraph{Niches.} Where a readout needs discrete niches, they are the ten clusters of a $k$-means clustering of the neighbour composition $\vy_i$ of connected cells; isolated cells have none.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} The mirror $R^2$ is the pooled coefficient of determination of a linear regression of $\vmu_z$ on the context $\vc_i$, both centred per type, on up to $30{,}000$ cells (\cref{sec:selection}). The response's information about the niche is a nearest-neighbour estimate \citep{ross2014} of the mutual information between the niche label and $\vmu_w$ on up to $20{,}000$ held-out cells, in nats, minus its mean over five permutations of the label within type. The cycle scores are the S and G2/M scores of the gene sets of \citet{tirosh2016}, intersected with the panel: for each set, the mean depth-normalised log expression of its genes minus that of control genes drawn from the same expression bins, computed with Scanpy \citep{wolf2018} from the cell's own segmented counts. They are a proxy for the cycle, not a measurement of it: the counts also hold transcripts leaked from neighbours and expression induced by the environment, and both enter the scores (\cref{sec:results-z}). The cycling set is the tenth of held-out cells with the highest summed score, fixed once per section for every method, together with the training cells that score above its lowest member; the cycle $R^2$ is that of a ridge regression of both scores on the latent, each centred per type within the set, fitted on the set's training cells and scored on its held-out cells. The linear reference is the same regression on the leading $50$ principal components of the log-normalised counts.

\paragraph{Transport.} A panel is a cell type and a pair of niches $A$ and $B$, each with at least $500$ cells that supply the model quantities and $100$ cells of the type to be scored. The tiles of the section are dealt into five spatial folds; one fold is scored, and the other four supply every model quantity. Per gene, the observed shift is the difference between the two niches in the logarithm of the type's mean depth-normalised counts. The prediction holds niche $A$'s intrinsic rate fixed and adds two channels: the response programmes, $\vB$ times the difference between the mean prior means $\mpsi(\vc_i, t)$ of the type's cells in $B$ and in $A$, and the leakage, the change in the logarithm of the mixture of \cref{eq:gen-p} when $A$'s mean foreign influx is replaced by $B$'s at the fit's $\kappa$. The prediction therefore moves the type-level prior mean to a new context with the intrinsic state held fixed; no cell's own posterior response enters it. Its score is the $R^2$ of the prediction against the observed shift, both centred over the genes expressed in both niches. The noise ceiling is the Spearman--Brown reliability of the observed shift, from the correlation between the shifts computed on two random halves of the scored cells, averaged over four splits. Only panels whose two niches differ by more than three standard deviations of composition along the line between them enter, and a panel is trusted when its ceiling is at least $0.5$ with at least $100$ genes scored. The fraction of the ceiling is the mean score over the trusted panels divided by their mean ceiling. Over all panels, trusted or not, the same ratio divides by near-zero ceilings and can exceed one, so it is reported only in \cref{tab:transport-heldout}. The programme and leakage parts of \cref{tab:cellina-cf} are the same ratio for each channel alone; they are separate scores and do not add up. The interval draws half of the tiles without replacement, recomputes the observed shifts, scores and ceilings on the drawn half and rescales the spread of the draws to the full section. On planted sections with tile-clustered noise its coverage is $0.94$ to $0.96$ at ceilings of $0.82$ and $0.85$, but $0.78$ to $0.88$ at ceilings of $0.52$ to $0.63$, just above the trusted tier's threshold, so at the ceilings of real panels it is closer to an $80\%$ than a $95\%$ interval.

\paragraph{Read A and the twin margin.} On the same panels, without the trust filter, up to $2{,}000$ held-out cells of the type in niche $A$ are decoded from their own $\vmu_z$ at niche $B$'s mean prior response and mean influx (the transported cloud) and at niche $A$'s own (the untransported cloud), and the type's held-out cells in $B$ are decoded at their own posterior means, context and influx (the target cloud). Read A is the gap closed, $(U - T)/(U - F)$, where $T$ and $U$ are the maximum mean discrepancies \citep{gretton2012} of the transported and the untransported cloud from the target, under a Gaussian kernel on square-root profiles, and $F$ that between two random halves of the target; it is clipped to $[-1, 1]$ and summarised by its median over panels. Its reference replaces the transported cloud by copies of the target's mean profile, and its interval resamples the tiles of the source cells. The twin margin matches each target cell to the transported source cell nearest to it in $\vmu_z$, and reports by how much the median Hellinger distance from the target's own decode to its match is smaller than to a random transported source cell. Because the decode is continuous in $\vz$, part of this margin follows from the construction.

\paragraph{Response programmes.} The programmes are the principal directions of the within-type-centred response that carry at least $1\%$ of its variance, rotated by varimax \citep{kaiser1958} in gene space; their number is the effective rank. The cross-seed cosine compares the first programme's gene signature between seeds, and the programme overlap of \cref{tab:breakdown-traj} compares the gene-space subspaces that the programmes of two fits span. For their localisation, each programme's absolute loadings over the genes expressed in at least $1\%$ of cells are compared between the members of four cellular-component sets of the Gene Ontology \citep{ashburner2000,go2023}, extracellular, plasma membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus, and the rest of the panel, by a Mann--Whitney test with Benjamini--Hochberg control \citep{benjamini1995} across sets and programmes within a fit; the effect is the rank-biserial correlation.

\paragraph{External criteria.} \emph{Signalling-gene share}, after MintFlow \citep{akbarnejad2025mintflow}: on the transport panels, each gene's observed shift is split into its response part, its leakage part and a remainder, and each gene's share of each part is accumulated over panels; the genes that are ligands or receptor subunits in a curated interaction database \citep{jin2025cellchat} are compared with the other genes of the panel by a Mann--Whitney test, with the rank-biserial correlation as the effect. \emph{Niche information of both latents}: the mutual information of the niche label with $\vmu_z$ and with $\vmu_w$ on $20{,}000$ held-out cells, by the nearest-neighbour estimator above and by a neural estimator \citep{belghazi2018}, each minus its mean over permutations within type. \emph{True and false axis}, after SIMVI \citep{dong2025simvi}, on the primary section only: cells are banded by the fraction of tumour cells among their $50$ nearest neighbours, in six bands. For each gene and type, the response-predicted shift per band, from the prior means of the type's cells, is correlated by Kendall's $\tau$ with the band index (the true axis) and with equal-count bins of the in-plane coordinate least correlated with the band index (the false axis). The same is done for shifts predicted from the intrinsic state and from depth, and for the observed shift. The false axis is not orthogonal to the bands: both in-plane coordinates correlate with the band index at about $0.41$.

\paragraph{Marker pairs.} After resolVI \citep{ergen2025}: twelve pairs of markers that should not be expressed together in one cell and six or seven control pairs that are, fixed per section before the read, are scored on held-out cells. A pair is double positive in the raw counts when both genes have a count; in the decode when both expected counts $\ell_i p_{ig}$ round to at least one; and in the corrected decode when both round to at least one after the expected foreign influx $\kappa\ell_i\bar\rho_{ig}$ is removed, that is, from $(1-\kappa)\,\ell_i\rho_{ig}$. The marker-pair contrast, reported as a trajectory in \cref{tab:breakdown-traj}, is the change in the exclusive pairs' double-positive rate from the raw counts to the corrected decode, minus the same change for the control pairs. At $\kappa = 0$ the corrected decode is the decode.

\subsection{Merged Subtypes}
\label{app:subtype}

Within each lineage that absorbed at least two curated classes or clusters, those become sublabels, each needing at least $20$ training and $10$ held-out cells. Probes fitted on training tiles and scored on held-out tiles predict the sublabel from $\vmu_z$, from $\vmu_w$, from the prior mean $\mpsi(\vc_i, t_i)$ and from both latents: a ridge regression (primary) and, for the two latents, a small perceptron. Each sublabel is scored by its one-versus-rest AUC against the rest of its lineage, with a floor from five permutations of the sublabels within the lineage and a tile-bootstrap interval. The call, fixed before the first read: a channel recovers a sublabel when the lower end of its interval is above its floor; channel $a$ is far ahead of $b$ when the interval of their difference is above zero and $b$'s excess over its floor is at most half of $a$'s; the call is the majority over seeds. Only the curated sublabels of the primary section and of the TMA core carry an expected allegiance (a state to $\vz$, a location to the response); the clusters of the lung and fresh-frozen sections are read for information. On the primary section the malignant cells lining the cyst are their own lineage, so every channel reads them through the type, and their expected allegiance cannot be tested. On the primary section, tumour- and stroma-associated fibroblasts and endothelial cells are separated by the response, and equally by its prior mean, with a one-versus-rest AUC of $0.96$ to $0.99$, against $0.71$ to $0.72$ from $\vz$, on two seeds of three by the pre-set call; proliferating and inflammatory tumour cells are separated by $\vz$ with $0.96$ to $0.97$, against $0.61$ from the response, on every seed. VEGFA$^+$ tumour cells, a state that also occupies a region, are read from $\vz$ ($0.96$) and also from the response ($0.82$). On the TMA core, myofibroblasts among adventitial fibroblasts are read better from the response than from $\vz$ ($0.92$ against $0.77$); on the fresh-frozen section, three tumour-state clusters are read from both ($0.90$ to $0.96$ from $\vz$, $0.77$ to $0.86$ from the response). On the ovarian FFPE and fresh-frozen sections the response and its prior mean give nearly the same AUC for every sublabel (within $0.07$ and $0.01$), the per-cell channel being closed (\cref{sec:results-w}). On the TMA core they do too (within $0.06$), except for activated against venous endothelium, which the prior mean separates better than the response ($0.76$ against $0.59$, on the one seed where it can be graded); few lineages there have enough cells to grade. On the lung section the response separates clusters within three lineages better than its prior mean (AT1, $0.67$ against $0.55$; multiciliated cells, $0.71$ against $0.57$; one tumour cluster, $0.73$ against $0.59$), although its own deviation adds no more to reconstruction there than elsewhere (\cref{tab:recon-modes}); $\vz$ separates all three better still ($0.90$). On that section the response readouts are therefore not only readouts of the context regression.

\subsection{What Each Part of the Model Buys in Reconstruction}
\label{app:recon-modes}

Held-out reconstruction decodes a cell from its own posterior, so it reads the cell's counts twice, once in the encoder and once in the likelihood, and a single number of it says little. \Cref{tab:recon-modes} instead decodes the held-out cells of each fit in four ways and reports the differences, in nats per count. The \emph{type profile} is the empirical count profile of the cell's type over training cells. The \emph{context} decode uses nothing of the cell's own counts: $\vz$ is its type's mean over training cells and $\vw$ the prior mean $\mpsi(\vc_i, t_i)$, so what remains is the type, the context and the foreign influx. The \emph{intrinsic} decode uses the cell's own $\vz$ with $\vw$ at the prior mean, and the \emph{full} decode its own $\vz$ and $\vw$. The foreign influx and $\kappa$ are those of the fit in every decode.

Three readings follow. The cell's own intrinsic state carries most of what the model adds over the type profile. The context, which uses no counts of the cell, adds a little on three sections and nothing on the lung FFPE section. And the response's own posterior adds almost nothing beyond its prior mean, one part in a thousand or less, which is the operating point's design: in reconstruction the response reads as the context regression (\cref{sec:objective}); on subtype recovery the lung section is the exception (\cref{app:subtype}). The model fitted to the TMA core reads its serial section as it reads the core.

\subsection{Per-Cell Divergence and Posterior Uncertainty}
\label{app:kl-maps}

For every cell of the final fits we computed the divergence of each posterior from its prior, $\KL(q(\vz_i)\,\|\,\N(\mathbf{0},\vI))$ and $\KL(q(\vw_i)\,\|\,p(\vw_i \mid \vc_i, t_i))$, and the mean posterior standard deviation of each (\cref{tab:kl-summary}). A divergence is the distance of the posterior from its prior, a standard deviation the posterior's own spread.

\paragraph{The response.} Its divergence is small, $0.004$ to $0.09$ nats per cell, and differs up to ten-fold between seeds of one section; its top decile holds $19$ to $43\%$ of the total. Within one fit it is spatially organised (Moran's I $0.15$ to $0.85$), but which cells deviate does not reproduce between seeds: the per-cell rank correlation between seeds is $-0.43$ to $0.57$ (\cref{fig:kl-seeds}). The top decile was compared with the other cells on cell type, niche, the share of neighbours of another type, degree, depth and held-out tile, under a rule fixed in advance: an odds ratio of at least $1.5$ or a standardised difference of at least $0.3$, with the interval clear of the null, in all three seeds. What passes is coarse: deviating cells avoid tumour on the two FFPE sections and lie among fibroblasts on the ovarian section and among chondrocytes on the TMA core; nothing passes on the fresh-frozen section, and no continuous feature passes anywhere. At the operating point the divergence is therefore not a per-cell anomaly score. Whether that reflects the tissue or the weight cannot be decided on tissue; on simulated sections, a planted per-cell response is not detected at this weight either, and goes partly to $\vz$ (\cref{app:planted-percell}). The posterior standard deviation of $\vw$ is $0.98$ to $1.01$, the prior's own scale: the response posterior is its prior in spread as well as in location.

\paragraph{The intrinsic state.} Its divergence and posterior standard deviation reproduce between seeds (rank correlations $0.78$ to $0.93$ and $0.53$ to $0.93$). Both depend on depth, deeper cells having smaller divergences and wider posteriors (pooled $\rho = -0.77$ to $-0.39$ and $0.21$ to $0.73$; weaker but of the same sign within types). This is the per-cell scaling of \cref{sec:objective}: relative to its own bound, a cell's divergence is weighted in proportion to its depth, so a deep cell is held closer to the prior and a sparse cell less. The intrinsic state is thus regularised less in low-count cells, and a deep cell's posterior width overstates its uncertainty, one more reason the reported uncertainty comes from the sweep and the seeds rather than from $q$.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the Assumptions}
\label{app:sensitivity}
\readorder{19}

\Cref{tab:sensitivity} refits the final configuration with one setting changed at a time, on the primary section and, for two of the families, on the TMA core and the fresh-frozen section, and gives each arm's move in units of the final configuration's standard deviation over its three seeds; \cref{fig:sensitivity} draws the moves. The arm without the intrinsic path, fitted on all four sections, is read against the final fits' range over seeds instead and is not in the table. An arm has two or three seeds, and the final configuration's spread over three seeds is itself a rough estimate, so moves of one or two such units are within what a change of seed can produce. Nothing is adopted from these arms.

\paragraph{The form of the leak.} In the three arms the leak rate is set per cell, in proportion to the ratio of its neighbours' depth, or density, to its own, capped, or per gene, in proportion to the gene's share of transcripts detected outside nuclei, each at the same mean rate as the section-wide $\kappa$. No read moves by more than $1.3$ standard deviations, and the transport read stays at $0.65$ to $0.66$. Within these three forms, then, the findings on the primary section do not depend on the leak being section-wide and gene-agnostic; forms outside them are not tested.

\paragraph{A false-positive floor.} The model has no background term (\cref{sec:generative}). A fixed false-positive floor added to the mixture, one rate per section or one per cell scaled by its area, leaves every diagnostic of the primary section within $0.6$ standard deviations, but lowers its transport read from $0.65$ to $0.50$ ($-8.3$) with the per-section floor and to $0.54$ ($-6.2$) with the area-scaled one, the largest moves in the table. On the TMA core the floor leaves transport at $0.73$ and moves the agreement with type up by $2.4$ and the mirror down by $4.0$ standard deviations of a very narrow spread. The transport read on the primary section therefore depends on the absence of a background term, and it is the read to treat with most caution there.

\paragraph{The response weight.} The operating point $\alphaw = 0.1$ lies far above the bound-equivalent value $1/\lbar$ (\cref{sec:objective}); it corresponds to $\alphaw = m/\lbar$ with $m$ of about $14$ on the primary section, $28$ on the TMA core and $143$ on the fresh-frozen section. Lowering it opens the per-cell channel. At $\alphaw = 1/\lbar$ the response's deviation from its prior mean carries $3.5\%$ of its variance on the primary section and $21\%$ on the TMA core, against $0.2\%$ at the operating point, and adds $0.015$ to $0.017$ nats per count to held-out reconstruction, against at most $0.001$. The cost is in the agreement of $\vz$ with type, which falls by $2.6$ and $2.8$ standard deviations ($0.684$ against $0.713$, $0.607$ against $0.621$), and by $2.9$ on the fresh-frozen section at $10/\lbar$ ($0.629$ against $0.697$). The lower weights raise the transport read on the primary section ($0.71$ at $1/\lbar$) and on the fresh-frozen section ($0.75$ at $5/\lbar$ and $10/\lbar$), but not on the TMA core. The operating point therefore keeps the agreement with type at the price of a closed per-cell channel (\cref{app:planted-percell}). The deviation's share of variance and its held-out gain are read from the same fits as \cref{tab:sensitivity} but are not in the table.

\paragraph{The adversary.} Doubling the heads' update steps or their width, or averaging an ensemble of three heads, changes the nonlinear composition residual by at most $0.7$ standard deviations, and doubling the steps costs $4.3$ in agreement with type. Weighting the composition term equally with the image term, the ablation of the adopted weight of three, raises the residual from $4.6\%$ to $6.6\%$ ($+2.5$) and the mirror by $2.0$; a weight of five lowers the residual to $3.7\%$ at a cost of $2.1$ in agreement with type.

\paragraph{Without the intrinsic path.} The final configuration is refitted on all four sections with $\omega = 0$, which drops the intrinsic path and sets the factor $(1+\omega)$ on the $\vz$-divergence to one, from three seeds at the final fits' splits, and read as the final fits are. The prediction was set before the fits: without the path the allocation should shift toward $\vw$, so that the agreement of $\vz$ with type and its cycle $R^2$ fall, the agreement of $\vmu_w$ with type and the cycle $R^2$ of $\vw$ rise, and the within-type share of $\vw$'s variance changes. A read counts as moved when the mean over the three seeds lies outside the final fits' range over seeds. Two reads move on every section, with the two ranges disjoint: the NMI of $\vmu_w$ with the type rises ($0.42$ to $0.50$ on the primary section, $0.27$ to $0.32$ on the lung section, $0.33$ to $0.35$ on the fresh-frozen section, $0.29$ to $0.40$ on the TMA core), and the within-type share of $\vw$'s variance falls ($0.32$ to $0.19$, $0.43$ to $0.37$, $0.53$ to $0.49$, $0.53$ to $0.33$). The intrinsic state loses less. Its cycle $R^2$ falls on the fresh-frozen section, from $0.76$ to $0.70$ with disjoint ranges; on the primary section its agreement with type ($0.71$ to $0.67$) and its cycle $R^2$ ($0.52$ to $0.50$) fall by the mean only, the ranges overlapping; elsewhere both stay within the final fits' range. The cycle $R^2$ of $\vw$ does not rise on any section, and no read for which nothing was predicted, the probe's composition residual, the response's information about the niche and held-out reconstruction among them, moves with disjoint ranges. No move is opposite to the prediction. Without the path, then, the type enters $\vw$, while $\vz$ keeps most of what it carries: the path's role at the final configuration is to keep the type out of $\vw$. One final fit on the TMA core, refitted with the software used for the ablation, comes out close to the original but not identical (NMI of $\vz$ $0.616$ against $0.621$, just below the final fits' range); the moves above are larger than that.
