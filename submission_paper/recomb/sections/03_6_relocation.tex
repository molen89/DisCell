\emph{Relocation} asks how a cell type's expression would change if its cells sat in another niche. DISCELL answers it by keeping each cell's intrinsic state, moving the prior of its response and its spill-over influx to the target niche, and decoding, so relocation exercises the whole model at once. For each type and pair of niches (a panel), the predicted change in the type's mean expression is scored against the observed difference between the two niches, as a share of the part of that difference that is reproducible between two random halves of the cells (the noise ceiling); only panels whose ceiling is high enough to be read count (\suppref{app:readouts}).

Moved to another niche, a cell type's predicted shift recovers $0.65$ to $0.73$ of the reproducible between-niche difference (\cref{tab:headline-full}). The response programmes of \cref{sec:programmes} carry most of it: predicted from the programmes alone, the shift recovers $0.52$ to $0.64$ of the ceiling, and from the spill-over alone $0.21$ to $0.39$ (the two parts are not additive; \cref{tab:cellina-cf}).

Cellina \citep{moeed2026cellina} also predicts expression under a rewired neighbourhood, so we score its counterfactual by the same reads on the same panels (\cref{tab:relocation}). The comparison is mixed. DISCELL's moved cells are closer to the target cells' own distribution on every section, with disjoint intervals on three, and DISCELL leads on all three reads on the FF section. Cellina recovers more of the ceiling on the ovarian FFPE section, and on the TMA core on its single readable panel. Scored on held-out tiles only (\cref{sec:setup}), DISCELL's share of the ceiling moves in both directions across fits and its single-cell read falls, while Cellina's share falls on both FFPE sections, on lung from $0.70$ to $0.49$, below DISCELL's range (\cref{tab:transport-heldout}).

A plain ridge regression of each type's expression on neighbour composition, fitted to the raw counts of the same panels, recovers $0.81$ to $0.89$ of the ceiling on the whole-slide sections, against DISCELL's $0.65$ to $0.72$, and is level on the TMA core (R in \cref{tab:relocation}); over all panels DISCELL is higher on three sections (\suppref{app:cellina-cf}). The observed shifts, however, contain the spill-over, so reproducing them rewards ignoring it. In simulation, where the spill-free shift between niches is known (\suppref{app:S-reloc-clean}), only DISCELL recovers it: its prediction from the programmes alone, without the spill-over, explains $R^2 \approx 0.76$ of the spill-free shift at every planted spill-over fraction from $0$ to $0.2$, while the regression falls from $0.40$ to $0.14$ as spill-over grows (log scale; below zero on the rate scale). DISCELL's full prediction falls too, by design, since it predicts the observed shift.
