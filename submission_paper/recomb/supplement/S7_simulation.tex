\section{Simulation}
\label{app:simulation}
\subsection{Synthetic Recovery}
\label{app:synthetic}

We check, on data simulated from the model's own generative story, that the final configuration recovers the planted intrinsic states, responses and gene programmes, that the adversary keeps the planted niche out of $\vz$, and how much a wrong spill-over fraction costs.

\paragraph{Simulation and fit.} $N = 6{,}000$ cells in a $1{,}000\um$ box with $K = 8$ spatially clustered types; the graph, kernel and composition built as for real data, with unit face lengths; $\vz_i \sim \N(\mathbf{m}_{t_i}, 0.5^2\vI)$; a planted response $\vw_i = \mathbf{M}^\top\vy_i + 0.3\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon_i$ linear in the neighbour composition ($R^2$ $0.75$ to $0.87$); $\log\vrho_i = \mathbf{A}\vz_i + \vB\vw_i$ (up to normalisation); the mixture at the planted $\kappa$; $\vx_i \sim \Mult(\ell_i, \vp_i)$ with $\ell_i \sim \max(\Pois(150), 20)$; and an eight-dimensional image descriptor $\vPhi_i = \mathbf{P}^\top\vy_i + 0.4\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon'_i$. $G = 60$ genes, planted $\dz = 4$, $\dw = 2$; three worlds at each planted $\kappa \in \{0, 0.2\}$, with no isolated cells. The fit is the final configuration (\cref{tab:architecture}) with widths reduced to the small world and $\alphaz = \tfrac12/\lbar$ at the simulated median count, three model seeds per world (nine fits per arm); the uncontrolled arm sets $\alphaa = 0$, and the misspecified arms fit the worlds planted at $\kappa = 0.2$ at each assumed $\kappa \in \{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$. The readouts are defined in the caption of \cref{tab:synthetic}; the loadings' reference is random two-dimensional subspaces of $\R^{60}$ (cosine mean $0.23$, $95$th percentile $0.37$ to $0.38$).

\paragraph{Result.} At the matched spill-over fraction every planted quantity is recovered on every world (\cref{tab:synthetic}). The agreement of $\vmu_z$ with the planted type is at or above what the counts support (below it in one fit of eighteen, $0.80$ against $0.83$) and short of the planted $\vz$, which $150$ counts per cell cannot reach. The loadings beat the random reference in every fit but vary between seeds on some worlds (one fit at $\kappa = 0$ reaches only $0.63$), which is why no gene-level statement on tissue rests on one fit (\cref{sec:sweep}). Without the adversary the intrinsic state carries the niche (composition $R^2$ $0.11$ to $0.19$ against at most $0.04$) and the response and loadings are recovered worse and less reliably. Fitted at any wrong $\kappa$ from $0$ to $0.4$ on worlds planted at $0.2$, recovery moves little (\cref{fig:synthetic-misspec}; canonical correlation lowest at an assumed $\kappa = 0$). Recovery therefore does not point to the planted spill-over fraction, and it says nothing about spill-over of a form the model cannot represent.

\subsection{A Planted Per-Cell Response}
\label{app:planted-percell}

At the operating point the response posterior adds about one part in a thousand of held-out reconstruction (\cref{tab:recon-modes}), and its per-cell divergence does not reproduce between seeds (\cref{app:kl-maps}). Either these tissues carry little per-cell response beyond what the niche predicts, or the weight $\alphaw = 0.1$ closes the channel whatever the data; on tissue the two cannot be told apart, so we plant one.

\paragraph{The plant.} On the three worlds planted at $\kappa = 0.2$, in $10\%$ of the cells of the most abundant type, drawn at random and so spread across niches, the log-rate is shifted along the first axis of the planted response programme, with norm $s$ times the size of the planted niche effect, for $s \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2\}$. The subset cannot be predicted from composition and the image descriptor (cross-validated AUC $0.44$ to $0.54$). The shift lies inside the response programme on purpose: it is a cell responding more or less strongly than its niche predicts, which is what the per-cell channel exists to carry. The fit is the final configuration unchanged, three seeds on world 0 and, as a replication outside the rule, on worlds 1 and 2.

\paragraph{The rule, fixed in advance.} Strength $s$ is detected if, on world 0, every seed separates the planted cells from the rest of their type by the response divergence $\KL(q(\vw_i)\,\|\,p(\vw_i \mid \vc_i, t_i))$ with an AUC of at least $0.8$, and every pair of seeds ranks the divergences with a Spearman correlation of at least $0.5$. If none were detected, that would be reported: at this weight the channel is closed by design, not by the data.

\paragraph{Result.} No strength is detected, on world 0 or on either replication (\cref{tab:planted-percell}). The divergence separates the planted cells only partly and not on every seed (AUC $0.66$ to $0.85$ at $s = 1$, $0.56$ to $0.69$ at $s = 2$ on world 0), and a plant does not make seeds agree more (Spearman $0.39$ to $0.41$ at $s = 0$, lower or similar under a plant; even the null misses the agreement criterion). The response's deviation from its prior mean moves toward the plant only weakly (correlation $0.11$ to $0.41$), with a held-out gain below $0.02$ nats per count on the planted cells. The plant goes increasingly to the intrinsic state instead: the correlation of $\vmu_z$ with the plant along the planted direction rises from at most $0.05$ at $s = 0$ to $0.36$ to $0.48$ (world 0), $0.86$ to $0.88$ (world 1) and $0.52$ to $0.70$ (world 2) at $s = 2$. At this weight the per-cell channel is therefore closed by design, not by the data: a per-cell response twice the size of the niche effect is not found in the response's divergence, and much of it goes to $\vz$. The absence of reproducible per-cell structure on tissue (\cref{app:kl-maps}) thus says nothing about whether the tissue has any; what opening the channel costs is in \cref{app:sensitivity}.

\subsection{The Amortisation Gap}
\label{app:planted}

The intrinsic encoder $q(\vz_i \mid \vx_i, t_i)$ does not read the spill-over influx $\rhobar_i$, so its posterior mean can differ from the exact $\E[\vz_i \mid \vx_i, t_i, \rhobar_i]$. This gap cannot be measured on tissue, so we measure it on simulated sections where the exact posterior is computable.

\paragraph{Simulation and fit.} DISCELL's generative model with the response switched off, so a gap is one of inference, not of model mismatch: $3{,}000$ cells in a $700\um$ square, four types in spatial patches; $\vz_i \sim \N(\mathbf{m}_{t_i}, 0.5^2\vI)$ in two dimensions and $\vrho_i = \softmax(\vz_i\mathbf{A})$ over forty genes; the model's kernel on a Delaunay graph pruned at $40\um$ ($\tau = 20\um$, unit faces) at $\kappa = 0.2$; depths $\ell_i \sim \Pois(D)$, at least $20$, for $D \in \{100, 300, 1{,}000\}$; and an image descriptor made from the neighbour composition plus noise. Given $\rhobar_i$ and the planted parameters, the two-dimensional posterior of $\vz_i$ is computed to numerical precision on a $129 \times 129$ grid. DISCELL is fitted with its response channel present, at the final configuration reduced to the small world, at the true $\kappa$ and at $\kappa$ off by up to $0.1$ either way, three seeds each ($45$ fits). The encoder's means are mapped onto the simulation's coordinates by a small cross-fitted network ($\vz$ is identified only up to a smooth invertible map), and a cell's gap is the distance to the exact mean in exact posterior standard deviations.

\paragraph{Result.} At the true $\kappa$ the encoder's gap is $0.65$, $1.02$ and $1.93$ posterior standard deviations at the three depths (\cref{tab:planted-gap}): above the perceptron trained on the same inputs at every depth (the encoder below it in only three of nine depth--seed combinations), $0.12$ to $0.58$ above the perceptron that also sees $\rhobar_i$, and well below the linear regression and the type mean. The gap grows with depth only in units of the narrowing posterior (standard deviation $0.17$ to $0.06$); in units of $\vz$ it stays at $0.16$ to $0.17$. A wrong $\kappa$ enlarges it by a factor of at most $1.13$, but the gap is not smallest at the true value at any depth (at $D = 1{,}000$, $\kappa$ too low by $0.1$ gives $1.77$ against $1.93$). At the development configuration (a closed-form penalty in place of the adversary, $600$ fixed epochs, every tile trained) the gap was smaller, $0.55$, $0.83$ and $1.48$, and below the perceptron's; whether the adversary or the stopping rule accounts for the difference has not been tested. The references have finite capacity and are not information bounds, so these statements are comparative.
