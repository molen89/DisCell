\subsection{The Response in Detail}
\label{app:response}

\paragraph{Relocation on held-out tiles.} \Cref{fig:transport-heldout,tab:transport-heldout} set the published relocation read beside the same read scored on the cells of the model's held-out tiles only. The published read is cross-fitted over spatial folds, but its folds are drawn from every tile, so most of its scored cells lie in the model's training tiles, whose counts the model saw. On held-out tiles every model quantity is still estimated from training tiles, and the panels, ceilings and mask are the same. The rule set before the read was to reconsider the headline read if the held-out fraction fell outside the published interval on any section; it did, in both directions, and the published read was kept, because an in-sample advantage would lower the held-out read throughout, and the fraction of the ceiling does not move that way. Read A does move that way, on $11$ of $12$ fits. The held-out pool is about three quarters of the published one, so fewer panels reach the trusted tier and several all-panel fractions exceed one; on the TMA core no panel is trusted on held-out tiles.

\paragraph{Localisation of the programmes.} The first response programme leans toward extracellular products on the ovarian FFPE section (rank-biserial effect $0.20$ to $0.22$), the lung section ($0.08$ to $0.16$) and the TMA core ($0.14$ to $0.20$), and away from nuclear ones ($-0.12$ to $-0.19$), in every seed; toward plasma-membrane products in eight of these nine fits ($0.10$ to $0.19$, and $0.03$, not significant, in the ninth). On the fresh-frozen section the extracellular effect is $0.17$, $0.06$ and $0.00$ in the three seeds: it fades to null rather than replicating. Since a gene-specific spill-over of transcripts near the membrane would produce the same lean, and $\kappa$ carries no gene index (\cref{sec:generative}), the lean describes the programmes and does not by itself distinguish a response from spill-over.

\paragraph{Hallmark labels of every programme.} \Cref{fig:hallmarks} shows the enrichment of all $50$ hallmark sets on all $34$ programmes of the twelve fits; $39$ sets are significant on at least one. EMT is significant on $30$ of the $34$ programmes, followed by oestrogen response early ($19$), KRAS signalling up ($17$), TNF$\alpha$/NF-$\kappa$B signalling and oestrogen response late ($15$ each) and coagulation ($14$), while $14$ of the $39$ sets are significant on at most two programmes.

\paragraph{Signalling genes.} In the spill-over channel, ligand and receptor genes take a larger share of the between-niche shift than other genes on every section and seed (rank-biserial effect $0.18$ to $0.28$, $p < 10^{-13}$). In the response channel they do on the ovarian FFPE section ($0.12$ to $0.17$), the lung section ($0.16$ to $0.18$) and the TMA core ($0.13$ to $0.16$), all with $p < 10^{-5}$, but not on the fresh-frozen section ($0.01$ to $0.03$, $p = 0.18$ to $0.61$). MintFlow's criterion is therefore met by the spill-over channel alone on every section. The response channel's lean holds across the spill-over grid on three sections and breaks down at $\kappa = 0.2$ on the fresh-frozen section (\cref{tab:breakdown}).

\paragraph{Niche information of both latents.} With the $k$-means niches, the response carries $0.41$ to $0.63$ nats of information about the niche beyond its floor and the intrinsic state $0.06$ to $0.11$, by the nearest-neighbour estimator, over the three seeds of all four sections; of the floor-corrected total, $85$ to $90\%$ lies in the response. The neural estimator gives the same picture. These estimates are on a different cell sample and floor from the guard of \cref{tab:headline}.

\paragraph{True and false axis.} On the primary section, the response-predicted shifts follow the tumour bands with a mean $|\tau|$ of $0.78$ to $0.81$ and the false axis with $0.51$ to $0.56$; at $|\tau| \geq 0.9$ that is $5{,}843$ to $6{,}314$ gene--type pairs on the true axis against $191$ to $1{,}100$ on the false one. By type, the contrast holds in tumour cells ($0.90$ to $0.93$ against $0.50$ to $0.63$) and in macrophages ($0.80$ to $0.81$ against $0.26$ to $0.34$) and is reversed in the merged fibroblast class ($0.63$ to $0.71$ against $0.71$ to $0.75$), whose stromal half lies in deep stroma at one end of the section, so that its false coordinate follows the bands. The references follow the bands less closely but show a contrast of similar size: shifts predicted from the intrinsic state reach $0.59$ to $0.65$ against $0.39$ to $0.47$, from depth $0.57$ to $0.68$ against $0.36$ to $0.42$, and the observed shift $0.47$ against $0.33$ to $0.36$. The response is thus the closest to the bands of all rows, but the band ordering is not specific to it.

\paragraph{Marker pairs.} On raw counts, exclusive double positives are $0.3$ to $2.2\%$ of held-out cells and control double positives $1.6$ to $11.8\%$. Decoding alone, at $\kappa = 0$, lowers the exclusive rate on every section, while the control rate rises on the lung ($4.4$ to $5.3\%$), fresh-frozen ($11.8$ to $14.1\%$) and TMA ($3.1$ to $4.6\%$) sections and falls on the ovarian FFPE section ($1.6$ to $1.0\%$), by more than the exclusive rate there ($0.5$ to $0.2\%$). This sets the sign of the contrast in \cref{tab:breakdown-traj}: negative on three sections, positive on the primary one. On those three sections the corrected decode lowers the exclusive rate and raises the control rate above its raw-count value at every grid value, except at the largest spill-over fractions on the lung ($\kappa = 0.3$ and $0.4$) and fresh-frozen ($\kappa = 0.4$) sections, where the control rate falls, by less than the exclusive rate. The spill-over correction adds a further fall at $\kappa > 0$: at $\kappa = 0.1$, the corrected decode lowers the exclusive rate below the uncorrected decode's by $0.10$ to $0.50$ percentage points and the control rate by $0.18$ to $1.13$, at least as much on every section, while the ratio of exclusive to control double positives falls on every section (for instance from $0.087$ to $0.028$ on the lung section). The correction therefore lowers double positives of both kinds, and exclusive ones relative to control ones, but the data do not show a removal specific to exclusive pairs. The corrected-decode rate falls steadily with $\kappa$, with no plateau, so it does not identify the spill-over fraction.

\subsection{Spatial Autocorrelation Within Types}
\label{app:moran}

\Cref{tab:moran} gives Moran's I of the posterior means within types. Each coordinate of $\vmu_z$ and $\vmu_w$ is centred on its type's mean, I is computed on the model's graph restricted to target cells with binary row-normalised weights, and the coordinates are averaged with weights equal to their within-type variance. The null permutes the centred values over cells; its $97.5$th percentile is at most $0.005$ on every section, so every entry is far above it. The response is spatially organised by construction, since it is largely a function of the context (\cref{app:pathb}). For the intrinsic state the table shows what the adversary removes and what it leaves. Without the adversary, I of $\vmu_z$ is $0.09$ to $0.47$; with it, $0.05$ to $0.35$, lower by $42$ to $48\%$ on three sections and by $25\%$ on the fresh-frozen one. What remains is within-type variation shared by neighbouring cells, which conditional invariance removes only to the extent that neighbour composition and the masked image predict it (\cref{app:conditional}). Without the adversary the response's posterior mean has almost no within-type variance: summed over its coordinates, below $10^{-9}$ in both fits of the fresh-frozen section, $10^{-5}$ and $0.02$ in the two of the ovarian FFPE section, and $0.03$ to $0.12$ on the lung and TMA sections, against $3.0$ to $39$ in the final fits. The intrinsic state carries the niche instead, and I of $\vmu_w$, which would be computed on what little variance is left, is reported for the final fits only.

\subsection{The Counterfactual Compared with Cellina}
\label{app:cellina-cf}

Cellina predicts a cell's expression under a rewired neighbourhood \citep{moeed2026cellina}, the counterfactual claim closest to our relocation read. For the comparison it is refitted on the training tiles only, the cells DISCELL is trained on, and decoded at its posterior mean; as for DISCELL, most of the cells the published read scores lie in its training tiles. Its prediction is then scored by our relocation reads on the panels, niches, ceilings and bootstrap draws of DISCELL's first seed (\cref{tab:cellina-cf}), so both are read as a cell type moved from one niche to another. Cellina has no spill-over channel, so its prediction cannot be split into programme and spill-over parts. The comparison is of one Cellina fit with one DISCELL seed; DISCELL's range over seeds, and both models scored on held-out tiles only, are in \cref{tab:transport-heldout}.

By the fraction of the noise ceiling, Cellina is ahead on the ovarian FFPE section ($0.73$ against $0.65$, intervals disjoint) and on the TMA core ($0.90$ against $0.71$, on a single trusted panel), and DISCELL is ahead on the fresh-frozen section ($0.68$ against $0.57$) and, with overlapping intervals, on the lung section ($0.74$ against $0.70$). DISCELL is ahead on Read A on all four sections, with overlapping intervals on the TMA core, and on the twin margin on three, by $0.04$ to $0.18$; on the lung section Cellina's is marginally larger ($0.435$ against $0.432$). Scored on held-out tiles only, Cellina's fraction of the ceiling stays above DISCELL's range on the ovarian FFPE section and falls below it on the lung section (\cref{tab:transport-heldout}).

\subsection{The Latents}
\label{app:latents}

The main text's \cref{fig:latents-main} shows the two latents on the primary section, and \cref{fig:latents-umap} the posterior means of one seed of the final configuration on each section, as UMAP. The intrinsic state separates the lineages (a). The response is organised by the cell's neighbours rather than by its own type alone (b). Within one type, the intrinsic state shows little grouping by the neighbours' lineage (c), while the response of the same cells does (d), which is what the conditional invariance asks for; the probe measures the same thing quantitatively (\cref{tab:probe}). The type of panels c and d is chosen by a rule, not by eye: among types with at least $3{,}000$ cells, the one whose neighbourhoods are most mixed. UMAP distorts distances and can exaggerate separation, so the figure illustrates what the probe and the agreement with type of \cref{sec:selection} measure, and replaces neither.

\subsection{Per-Cell Divergence and Posterior Uncertainty}
\label{app:kl-maps}

For every cell of the final fits we computed the divergence of each posterior from its prior, $\KL(q(\vz_i)\,\|\,\N(\mathbf{0},\vI))$ and $\KL(q(\vw_i)\,\|\,p(\vw_i \mid \vc_i, t_i))$, and the mean posterior standard deviation of each (\cref{tab:kl-summary}). A divergence is the distance of the posterior from its prior, a standard deviation the posterior's own spread.

\paragraph{The response.} Its divergence is small, $0.004$ to $0.09$ nats per cell, and differs up to ten-fold between seeds of one section; its top decile holds $19$ to $43\%$ of the total. Within one fit it is spatially organised (Moran's I $0.15$ to $0.85$), but which cells deviate does not reproduce between seeds: the per-cell rank correlation between seeds is $-0.43$ to $0.57$ (\cref{fig:kl-seeds}). The top decile was compared with the other cells on cell type, niche, the share of neighbours of another type, degree, depth and held-out tile, under a rule fixed in advance: an odds ratio of at least $1.5$ or a standardised difference of at least $0.3$, with the interval clear of the null, in all three seeds. What passes is coarse: deviating cells avoid tumour on the two FFPE sections and lie among fibroblasts on the ovarian section and among chondrocytes on the TMA core; nothing passes on the fresh-frozen section, and no continuous feature passes anywhere. At the operating point the divergence is therefore not a per-cell anomaly score. Whether that reflects the tissue or the weight cannot be decided on tissue; on simulated sections, a planted per-cell response is not detected at this weight either, and goes partly to $\vz$ (\cref{app:planted-percell}). The posterior standard deviation of $\vw$ is $0.98$ to $1.01$, the prior's own scale: the response posterior is its prior in spread as well as in location.

\paragraph{The intrinsic state.} Its divergence and posterior standard deviation reproduce between seeds (rank correlations $0.78$ to $0.93$ and $0.53$ to $0.93$). Both depend on depth, deeper cells having smaller divergences and wider posteriors (pooled $\rho = -0.77$ to $-0.39$ and $0.21$ to $0.73$; weaker but of the same sign within types). This is the per-cell scaling of \cref{app:pathb}: relative to its own bound, a cell's divergence is weighted in proportion to its depth, so a deep cell is held closer to the prior and a sparse cell less. The intrinsic state is thus regularised less in low-count cells, and a deep cell's posterior width overstates its uncertainty, one more reason the reported uncertainty comes from the sweep and the seeds rather than from $q$.

\subsection{Training Cost}
\label{app:timing}

\Cref{tab:timing} gives the cost of a fit. DISCELL's time per epoch is pure training, measured over twenty epochs with no evaluation. Its wall-clock time covers the three final fits, including evaluations every five epochs and the early stop. The comparison methods' times are the training time of the fits compared in this paper, at lineage labels, as recorded when they were run. MintFlow's whole-section fit of the lung FFPE section trained for $879$ minutes, $14.8$ hours of wall-clock time with its set-up and prediction, against $3.2$ to $7.4$ minutes for a whole DISCELL fit of the same section. Its fit of the ovarian FFPE section trained for $939$ minutes, $15.8$ hours of wall-clock time, with a peak of $9.5$ GB of GPU and $23.6$ GB of host memory. The other whole-section fits of SIMVI and MintFlow could not be run on the resources currently available; the measured reasons are in \cref{tab:baselines,tab:timing}.

In symbols, a training step on a tile of $n$ seeds with $n_1$ cells in ring one costs $O((n + n_1)\,G h)$ in the encoders and decoder, for hidden width $h$, and $O(|E_n|\,G)$ in the spill-over influx, for the $|E_n|$ edges into the seeds (\cref{app:tiles}). The six adversary head updates add $O\big(6\,(n + n_1)\,h_a(\dz + K)\big)$ with $h_a = 64$, small beside the gene-sized terms because $\dz$ and $K$ are far below $G$. An epoch visits every training tile once, so its cost is linear in the number of cells; the held-out probes are fitted on at most $30{,}000$ cells whatever the section's size. Peak memory is the section's count matrix, held resident, plus the gathered edge-by-gene rows of the influx, $O(|E_n|\,G)$ per step, which the tile size bounds. The spill-over sweep multiplies the cost of a fit by its six grid points and three seeds, eighteen fits per section. Sample size is not analysed: how many cells a section needs for the readouts to stabilise is not studied beyond the range of sizes in \cref{tab:sections}.
